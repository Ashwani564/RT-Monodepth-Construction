CLUSTER 1: Advancements in YOLO Architectures and Edge Computer Vision
------------------------------------------------------------------------

[1] YOLOv11-Based Segmentation for Cluttered Environments
He, L., Zhou, Y., Liu, L., & Ma, J. (2024). Research and application of YOLOv11-based object
segmentation in intelligent recognition at construction sites. Buildings, 14(12), 3777.
https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings14123777
Paragraph: To address the intricate spatial relationships on highly cluttered construction sites,
recent object detection research has pivoted toward advanced segmentation capabilities. He et al.
(2024) demonstrated the utility of YOLOv11-based object segmentation algorithms for intelligent
recognition tasks on active sites. By accurately masking the complex outlines of heavy machinery
and personnel rather than relying solely on coarse bounding boxes, this architecture significantly
minimizes background noise and improves hazard identification accuracy in chaotic construction
environments.

[2] YOLOv12 for Tracking Dangerous Driving Behaviors
Ge, T., Ning, B., & Xie, Y. (2025). YOLO-AFR: An improved YOLOv12-based model for accurate and
real-time dangerous driving behavior detection. Applied Sciences, 15(11), 6090.
https://doi.org/10.3390/app15116090
Paragraph: The continuous progression of YOLO architectures offers critical enhancements for
tracking dynamic heavy equipment operations. Ge et al. (2025) proposed YOLO-AFR, an optimized
YOLOv12-based model designed specifically for real-time dangerous driving behavior detection. In
the context of heavy machinery operators, actively tracking behaviors such as distraction, fatigue,
or single-handed driving provides a preventative layer to safety management, intercepting
human-factor risks before they manifest into severe struck-by or collision incidents.

[3] State-of-the-Art CNN Deployments in Construction
Cai, R., Li, J., Tan, Y., Tang, J., & Chen, X. (2025). Convolutional neural networks for
construction safety: A technical review of computer vision applications. Applied Soft Computing,
180, 113374.
Paragraph: The foundational role of deep learning in modern safety frameworks is undisputed, but
the field requires continual synthesis to benchmark new capabilities. In their comprehensive
technical review, Cai et al. (2025) mapped the latest convolutional neural network (CNN)
applications deployed specifically for construction safety monitoring. The review confirms that
state-of-the-art computer vision systems now possess the robustness necessary to transition from
academic prototypes to ubiquitous, continuously operational site-surveillance tools capable of
mitigating real-time hazards.

[4] Optimized Helmet Detection in Occluded Environments
Zhang, L., Sun, Z., Tao, H., Liu, Y., Guo, X., & Chen, J. (2024). Research on mine-personnel
helmet detection based on multi-strategy-improved YOLOv11. Sensors, 25(1), 170.
Paragraph: Ensuring proper personal protective equipment (PPE) compliance in harsh environments
with extreme lighting variability — such as tunneling or night-shift construction — poses a
significant algorithmic challenge. Zhang et al. (2024) developed a multi-strategy improved
YOLOv11 model specifically tuned for helmet detection in dark and occluded environments. Their
findings illustrate how targeted architectural optimizations, including advanced attention
mechanisms and enhanced feature extraction, can overcome severe environmental constraints to
maintain high precision in PPE enforcement.

[5] Real-World Efficacy of CV Safety Applications
Vasquez, E., Hale, M., & Chen, S. (2025). Computer vision applications for real-time safety
monitoring on construction sites. ResearchGate.
Paragraph: The integration of computer vision extends beyond simple object detection into the
continuous enforcement of occupational health standards. Vasquez et al. (2025) analyzed
field-deployed computer vision applications designed to flag unsafe conditions and proactively
prevent accidents. By documenting real-world implementations, this work underscores how automated
visual analytics transition safety protocols from a reactive, retrospective posture into a
continuous, real-time preventative operation, offering immediate alerting capabilities to site
managers.


CLUSTER 2: Progress in Monocular Depth Estimation (MDE)
---------------------------------------------------------

[6] Resolving Scale Ambiguity for Construction Topographies
Shen, J., Yan, W., Qin, S., & Zheng, X. (2023). A self-supervised monocular depth estimation
model with scale recovery and transfer learning for construction scene analysis. Computer-Aided
Civil and Infrastructure Engineering, 38(9), 1142-1161.
Paragraph: Translating 2D pixel detections into actionable 3D proximity alerts requires bridging
the gap between relative depth and absolute real-world metric distance. Shen et al. (2023) tackled
this by proposing a self-supervised monocular depth estimation model specifically calibrated for
construction scene analysis. By integrating scale recovery mechanisms and transfer learning, their
framework provides the critical metric distances needed to trigger safety alarms when workers
encroach upon the operational radius of heavy machinery, achieving this spatial awareness using
only a single standard camera.

[7] Systematizing the Transition to MDE
Zhang, Z., Zhang, Y., Li, Y., & Wu, L. (2025). Review of monocular depth estimation methods.
Journal of Electronic Imaging, 34(2), 020901.
https://doi.org/10.1117/1.JEI.34.2.020901
Paragraph: The methodological shift from stereo vision to monocular perception relies heavily on
recent advancements in neural network architectures. Zhang et al. (2025) provide an exhaustive
review categorizing the evolution of monocular depth estimation methods from early geometry-based
techniques to contemporary deep learning pipelines. Their synthesis clarifies how modern
data-driven approaches circumvent the need for complex, expensive sensing hardware, making
ubiquitous 3D spatial monitoring highly feasible on resource-constrained, temporary construction
sites.

[8] Asynchronous Spatial Memory for Edge Processing
Ma, L., Li, Y., Jiang, B., Zhong, Z., Ding, H., & Zhu, L. (2026). AsyncMDE: Real-time monocular
depth estimation via asynchronous spatial memory. arXiv preprint arXiv:2603.10438.
Paragraph: A major constraint in deploying highly accurate depth estimation models on construction
sites is their intensive computational demand. Ma et al. (2026) addressed this limitation by
introducing AsyncMDE, an innovative asynchronous depth perception system designed to amortize high
computational costs. By utilizing a lightweight foreground model that continuously refreshes spatial
features generated by a heavier background foundation model, AsyncMDE dramatically reduces
parameter requirements, enabling sustained, high-frame-rate spatial perception on resource-limited
edge processors.

[9] Real-Time Deployment Constraints and RTS-Mono
Cheng, Z., Liu, T., Lei, T., Hua, X., Zhang, Y., & Tang, C. (2025). RTS-Mono: A real-time
self-supervised monocular depth estimation method for real-world deployment. arXiv preprint
arXiv:2511.14107.
Paragraph: Deploying predictive safety alerts relies heavily on maintaining high inference speeds
on local hardware to ensure near-zero latency. Cheng et al. (2025) developed RTS-Mono, an
ultra-lightweight self-supervised monocular depth estimation architecture optimized specifically
for real-world deployment. With a parameter count drastically lower than traditional foundation
models, RTS-Mono successfully preserves spatial fidelity while achieving real-time inference
speeds of up to 49 frames per second on standard embedded edge devices, making it highly suitable
for active site monitoring.

[10] Cross-Domain Applications from Autonomous Driving
Huang, Z., Ali, M. A. H., Nukman, Y., Xu, H. L., Zhang, S., Chen, H., & Alkhedher, M. (2025).
A systematic review of monocular depth estimation for autonomous driving: Methods and dataset
benchmarking. Results in Engineering, 26, 105359.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rineng.2025.105359
Paragraph: Much of the innovation propelling spatial awareness in construction originates from the
autonomous driving sector. Huang et al. (2025) systematically reviewed monocular depth estimation
methods tailored for autonomous vehicle navigation, benchmarking algorithmic performance across
diverse, complex datasets. The computational techniques utilized to protect pedestrians from moving
vehicles on public roads map directly onto the challenges of preventing struck-by incidents on
construction sites, offering a rich source of algorithmic cross-pollination for construction safety
engineers.


CLUSTER 3: Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal AI Integration
----------------------------------------------------------------------

[11] Vision-Language Models for Unstructured Visual Reasoning
Adil, M., Lee, G., Gonzalez, V. A., & Mei, Q. (2025). Using vision language models for safety
hazard identification in construction. arXiv preprint arXiv:2504.09083.
Paragraph: While traditional computer vision identifies predefined object classes, newer multimodal
frameworks promise a deeper semantic understanding of complex hazard scenarios. Adil et al. (2025)
evaluated the efficacy of Vision Language Models (VLMs) applied directly to safety hazard
identification tasks. By leveraging the immense contextual reasoning capabilities of VLMs, this
approach transcends basic bounding box generation, allowing safety systems to interpret nuanced,
unstructured visual data and articulate context-specific safety guidelines.

[12] Grounding Visual AI with RAG Frameworks
Wang, C., et al. (2025). Automating construction safety inspections using a multi-modal
vision-language RAG framework. arXiv preprint arXiv:2510.04145.
Paragraph: To address the hallucination vulnerabilities often seen in generative AI, researchers
are increasingly grounding foundation models in strict regulatory data. Wang et al. (2025)
introduced SiteShield, a multi-modal Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) framework designed to
automate construction safety inspection reports. By integrating visual site observations with
retrieved text from official OSHA guidelines, this system produces highly accurate, contextually
relevant safety assessments, significantly outperforming standard unimodal language models in
formal compliance checks.

[13] Optimizing Knowledge Retrieval Architecture
Lee, J., Ahn, S., Kim, D., & Kim, D. (2024). Performance comparison of retrieval-augmented
generation and fine-tuned large language models for construction safety management knowledge
retrieval. Automation in Construction, 168, 105846.
Paragraph: The sheer volume of regulatory codes and internal safety protocols requires intelligent
information retrieval systems to aid site managers effectively. Lee et al. (2024) systematically
compared the performance of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) against fine-tuned Large Language
Models for querying construction safety management knowledge. Their findings provide a crucial
architectural blueprint for developing reliable AI assistants, demonstrating how fetching
deterministic regulatory text prior to generation yields far more reliable compliance guidance than
relying on parameterized model memory.

[14] Mapping the Landscape of AI in Occupational Safety
Badhan, S. J., & Samsami, R. (2025). Artificial intelligence (AI) in construction safety: A
systematic literature review. Buildings, 15(22), 4084.
Paragraph: The rapid proliferation of computational tools in the built environment necessitates a
holistic understanding of AI's current trajectory. In their systematic literature review, Badhan
and Samsami (2025) cataloged the diverse applications of artificial intelligence within
construction safety. Their work highlights the critical transition from passive data collection to
proactive, AI-driven risk mitigation, while simultaneously identifying gaps in longitudinal
deployment studies, thereby charting a clear course for future socio-technical safety research.

[15] Prompt Engineering for Construction Diagnostics
Samsami, R. (2024). Optimizing the utilization of generative artificial intelligence (AI) in the
AEC industry: ChatGPT prompt engineering and design. CivilEng, 5(4), 971-1010.
Paragraph: As natural language interfaces become the primary medium through which personnel
interact with complex safety telemetry, the methodology of querying these models dictates the
quality of the output. Samsami (2024) explored the vital role of advanced prompt engineering and
design when deploying generative AI, such as ChatGPT, within the Architecture, Engineering, and
Construction (AEC) industry. By establishing structured scaffolding and domain-specific prompting
protocols, practitioners can drastically improve model reasoning reliability when parsing safety
logs or generating operational workflows.
